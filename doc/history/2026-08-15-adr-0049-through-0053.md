# ADR 0049 through 0053 — 2026-08-15

One round: four decisions built, one answered and not built, run as parallel worktrees
plus a gate replacement that came out of watching the suite. Not edited after the fact; a
correction is a later entry.

---

**A tile is not a window on a wider region unless the rasteriser cuts at its border**
(ADR 0049, first half).

The round set out to stop re-rasterising a clip chain's residue once per clipped command,
by rasterising it once over the chain's region and cropping per command. The probe written
to check that cropping was legitimate found that it was not: `fill_mask` clamped the two
endpoints of an edge piece that left the region and interpolated between them. The row's
*total* winding survived — every column past the crossing read the right value, which is
why no test in either tree had ever seen it — while the columns **at** the border took the
height the piece spent outside. **2 684 differing pixels of 2 863 228, worst by 185 of
255.** Cutting the piece at the border instead leaves 31 pixels differing, every one by 1
of 255, which is `f32` addition not being associative and nothing structural.

The test states its expected value from the geometry — 0.625 of a pixel of area is 159 of
255 — rather than from any rasteriser. The tree was producing 128. The fast path, a piece
wholly inside the region, is bit-identical to before, so only tiles that a clip or the page
edge cuts in x can move at all.

**A clip chain's residue is rasterised once, over the region it occupies** (ADR 0049,
second half, `examples/residue_clip.rs`, `encode/residue.rs`).

Keyed by the chain's deepest non-rectangular link, which under one viewport determines the
region; admitted only when `region ≤ uses × tile` and when it fits a quarter of
`max_frame_bytes`, both checked before allocation. Artwork — the corpus's p99 clip shape —
goes **geometry 37.78 → 28.89 ms, encode 46.26 → 37.17, wall 51.72 → 40.35**, base and
change alternating, three rounds each, minima, load 3.8–4.8. 600 residue rasterisations
became 185. The same pair measured at load 30 read the wrong way round by 60 %, which is
`HANDOVER.md`'s first trap arriving live.

Two of the numbers this round was *briefed* with were wrong and the work corrected them.
The seam was not "35 ms of a 43 ms frame": the residue span is 17.3 ms of the 65.6 ms
artwork spends flattening and rasterising, about a quarter. And the corpus refuses **two**
pages on `ScratchExhausted`, not four, with the other refusals a different budget or a
correct clause refusal each — so "the only reason any frame of the corpus is refused" had
been too strong for some time.

A budget overrun here **declines the cache** rather than returning an `Err`, against the
instruction the round was given. Refusing a drawable frame because a cache filled up is
principle 6 pointed the wrong way, there is a fallback that draws the identical page, and
the atlas has said so since ADR 0029. The deviation was argued and kept.

**A repack that would rebuild the layout it replaces is not taken** (ADR 0050).

The premise this was briefed with — a page whose glyph tiles overflow the atlas re-encodes
every frame, because the repack bumps the generation inside `EncodeKey` — was **too broad,
and the sweep is what showed it.** Seventeen (atlas budget, magnification) pairs, twelve
retained frames each, reading the encode-source sequence: sixteen settled after one encode;
exactly one never did. The pathology is the narrow band where a page fits the atlas **by
bytes** and not **by shelves** — ADR 0024 gated the repack on `requested_bytes <=
atlas_bytes` while the packer allocates shelves and wastes two remainders. The reproducing
row asks for 63 % of the atlas by area and five of its 107 tiles do not fit.

Keying on the generation the encode *finished* with was considered and refused as actively
wrong: it is a no-op today, and if a reset ever could happen mid-encode that encode is
broken in its middle whatever the key says. The fix is a third condition — the atlas must
hold entries this frame did not use — with a proof rather than a heuristic behind it:
re-packing an atlas that holds nothing else replays the identical `allocate` sequence and
reproduces the layout tile for tile, including the tile that overflows. Bound: at most one
repack, two encodes, then replays for ever.

**A secondary finding, from auditing ADR 0048's key rather than inheriting it.**
`allocate_id` was `wrapping_add` on a `u32` shared by four resource families. After 2³²
uploads an id is reissued, the live resource is silently replaced, and `generation` does not
move because it counts *releases* — so a retained encode would draw bytes it never named
with every `EncodeKey` field comparing equal. Unreachable in practice, never written down,
and now `DeviceError::ResourceIdsExhausted`.

**Three files stop holding three responsibilities each** (ADR 0051).

`scene.rs` 1 216 → 7 files (largest 378), `compose.rs` 1 014 → 6 (largest 329),
`winding.rs` 844 → 4 (largest 384), against CLAUDE.md's ~500-line smell. None of the three
turned out to be irreducible, and two announced their own seams in their module comments —
`scene.rs` had a heading reading *"Validation is here, and it is loud"* sitting among
headings about what a scene *is*.

Behaviour-preserving, and checked rather than asserted: the public item set of
`quorra-scene::scene` is identical before and after, and a 39-command page through every
lane — glyph fills at 24 sub-pixel phases, a residue clip chain, alpha and luminosity
masks, a staged `DestOut` inside a knockout group, a non-isolated group, shading, images,
stroke — hashes identically at two scales on both coverage lanes.

The one judgement call recorded: `WindingTexture` stays with the two passes rather than
taking its own file, because the invariant that makes it correct — *the pane is the
top-left of a texture kept from a larger frame* — is stated by neither the texture nor
either pass alone, and that is the caller's `QUORRA_FEEDBACK.md` §11 defect.

**A gate whose threshold sits above the regression it names is replaced by a count**
(ADR 0052).

`a_readback_frame_does_not_pay_for_its_pixels_twice` asserted `< 6 ms` against a regression
whose measured value is **3.84 ms**. The shape ADR 0022 removed would have passed it on any
quiet machine; the only thing it ever failed for was this desktop's load, two runs in five.
It had been that way for three ADRs, and it was found by watching the suite flap rather
than by reading the gate.

Two ratio normalisers were measured before any threshold was proposed — readback ÷ (frame −
readback) gave 1.17–9.12 on unchanged code, readback ÷ an 8 MB host pass gave 0.48–1.84 —
so no constant there would have been evidence of anything.

The claim splits along its own seam: *"reads once"* is a count and is exact, *"divides
never"* is a duration this machine cannot measure. `tests/readback_cost.rs` asserts the
count with a `#[global_allocator]` in the test crate — one allocation of a megabyte or
more, of exactly the raster's bytes. **Verified in both directions before it was written
down**: 1 allocation / 8 022 576 bytes as the tree stands, 2 / 16 213 552 with
`read_buffer`'s staging `Vec` put back, identical on RADV and llvmpipe across nine runs at
load 7–30. The divide half is now un-gated and says so; its instrument is callgrind.

The cost is the first `unsafe` in this tree — one `unsafe impl GlobalAlloc` forwarding every
method to `System`, in a test crate never linked into the viewer, on principle 3's own
stated terms.

**A paint the device evaluates — asked, measured, answered, not built** (ADR 0053,
`examples/function_paint/`, `doc/spike-function-paint.md`,
`doc/research-function-paint-arithmetic.md`).

The caller asked for a §7.10.5 function evaluated per fragment. A full page of their
seven-segment witness: **4 988 ms on their processor, 0.060 ms as a generated shader on
RADV**, 1.97 ms on llvmpipe. The interpreter shape they expected to win the startup
property lost on both axes — 133.7 ms per page, 596 ms to 4.5 s of cold compile against
6.3 ms — and at 4× it lost the device outright to a hard recovery.

The agreement question came out better than either side expected and for a reason neither
had stated: **zero differing pixels** over four million pixels of discontinuous function,
because neither witness calls a transcendental. Our own probe shows the danger is real for
the ones that do — our two adapters disagree bitwise on `sin`, `cos`, `exp`, `sqrt`, `div`
and `atan`, and `sin 0 ge` flips between them on 2 of 4 096 inputs — so the answer is a
static classification rather than any of the three tolerances the ask offered.

ISO 32000-2 supplies no precision contract at all: §7.3.3 defers to the machine, its Annex C
is informative, "IEEE 754" appears exactly twice in the document and never normatively, and
§7.10.5.2's PLRM3 defers again. That silence is recorded as a silence.

**The round's scaffolding is removed in the same commit.** Three `round-notes-*.md` and two
`api-change-*.md` were written by parallel agents as transfer documents, because a round run
in three worktrees cannot have three writers on `PLAN.md`. Every line of them is now in
`PLAN.md`, `HANDOVER.md`, this file, or the caller's own
`doc/QUORRA_API_2026_08_15.md`, and a lesson belongs in exactly one place. They are in git
history if a claim needs its origin.

**A §7.10.5 program is uploaded, admitted, generated into a shader and drawn** (ADR 0053
built, `src/function/`, `src/pipeline/function.rs`, `src/encode/function.rs`,
`src/compose/function.rs`, `crates/quorra-function-conformance`).

Run as two waves of parallel worktrees against a vocabulary the integrator pinned. **Both
defects in that pin were the integrator's, and both were invisible on the caller's corpus.**
The first: the pin told the generator to clamp `(x, y)` into the domain, where §8.7.4.5.2
requires points outside the transformed domain rectangle to take `Background` or be left
unpainted — a clamp smears the edge colour at full alpha across the gap, and revision 1 had
no field that could express the correct answer. The second: `Paint::Function(Arc<…>)` cost
`Paint` its `Copy` and broke thirteen sites, where a `FunctionId` handle — the shape every
other heavy paint here already has — keeps it and moves admission to `Device::upload_function`,
before a frame exists. Both witnesses declare `/Domain [0 1 0 1]`, so no corpus run could
have found either.

**Two clause defects were found in wave-1 code by wave 2, both by the corpus rather than by
reading.** §7.10.5.3's output count is an equality ("shall be an error … to *differ*") and
was implemented as a floor, so a refusal ground existed that nothing enforced. And `true 1
eq` returned true: on the operand stack a boolean *is* `1.0f`, so a numeric comparison
inverted PLRM3's rule that a boolean and a number are never equal — a wrong colour that no
existing test could see. Both gates are verified able to fail.

**`Agreement::Exact` was renamed `Bounded`**, and the ADR carries the amendment. WGSL
§15.7.5 permits reassociation and fusion, so bit-exactness is not available even for `add`,
`sub` and `mul`; ADR 0006's store rounding sits downstream regardless. The spike's
zero-differing-pixels result was real and was an observation about two documents on one
driver, and the ADR had promoted it to a guarantee. Two agents raised it independently while
implementing it. The claim that survives is the one §10.7.3 already measures a shading's
accuracy in: the disagreement stays a difference of colour rather than of branch.

385 tests pass on **both** adapters. The 125-case corpus runs on the device against an
independently written reference evaluator — 91 compared, 73 of them bitwise, 30 carrying no
value because the standard supplies none. The corpus changed by nothing: 931/23/2/18, every
page line identical to the pre-round run.

**The geometry phase divides, and the order does not** (ADR 0054, `encode/parallel.rs`,
`examples/encode_threads.rs`, `tests/encode_threads.rs`).

The caller asked for `encode` on more than one thread and stated their own ceiling doing it.
Measured on our side first, because **their page shape was not in our archetype set** —
`giant` flattens 8 segments for an 80-pixel tile where theirs flattens 52 for nine, so every
number we had described a different kind of page. `drawing` is the seventh archetype now.
Their 79.2 % geometry share reproduces at 76 % here, and geometry is the largest phase of an
encode on every shape we have.

A serial walk records a job, a parallel pass rasterises touching no frame state, a serial
commit charges and packs in encounter order. `std::thread::scope`, **no dependency and no
`unsafe`**; `Options::encode_threads` defaults to 1 and is a permission rather than a
preference. Order-dependence is handled *structurally* — every route to an order-dependent
effect drains the queue first — because a rule that depends on remembering does not survive
editing.

Their page: geometry **309.0 → 46.9 ms** and encode **406.8 → 132.2** at 24 threads. artwork
is 1.2× because 600 of its 684 marks are residue-clipped and stay serial, and that is stated
rather than averaged away. An earlier round at load 25–33 read 24 threads as *worse* than 8,
so no crossover is published as a constant.

**The determinism evidence is the part worth keeping.** Equal bytes and equal counters at 1,
2, 3, 7 and 64 threads over four fixtures — and then **the caller's 956 real pages at 1
thread against 8, every per-page line identical**, scale 4 unmoved. The gate was verified
able to fail in two directions, and it found a real defect nobody had read: a duplicate
atlas insert, `bytes_uploaded` off by exactly 64. The first fixture written for it used a
lattice where nothing overlapped and passed with an ordering drain removed; that is now a
trap in this file.

`recording` is the largest phase of that page afterwards, at 132 ms of encode against
geometry's 47 — so ADR 0023's "revisit when" is closer, and the caller excluded recording
from their ask deliberately.
